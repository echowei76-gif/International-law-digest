Mainstream / Institutionalist

The proceedings are a normal exercise of ICJ jurisdiction and admissibility doctrine: Germany's preliminary objections raise technical questions of consent, temporal limitation (ratione temporis), and the Monetary Gold principle, which the Court must resolve before reaching the merits. Third-state responsibility for arms transfers is governed by established secondary rules, including Article 16, and the fault threshold for complicity is higher than for prevention, so the legal risk to supplying states is real but bounded and litigated through ordinary judicial channels.

AI and International Law: Sovereignty, Due Diligence, and State Responsibility in the Digital Age

Scholarship and institutional reports are exploring how AI interacts with state sovereignty, due diligence, and state responsibility, including whether designating AI tools and infrastructure as critical infrastructure triggers obligations of risk mitigation and notification. The ILC has been advancing work on due diligence as a concept at the interface of primary rules and secondary rules of state responsibility, and the OHCHR has issued a report on human rights due diligence for AI. Debate continues over whether a positive obligation to regulate AI can be grounded in existing international law.

Mainstream / Institutionalist

Existing international law, including the ILC Articles on State Responsibility and human rights due diligence standards, already provides a workable framework for AI governance. States have obligations to respect, protect, and fulfil human rights, and due diligence requires systemic and continuing risk assessment; the precautionary principle can support regulatory action, and international cooperation, open standards, and shared research are necessary because no state can build every AI component alone.

Sovereignty over Plant Genetic Resources and Food Security: The ITPGRFA Package Deal and TWAIL Critiques

The International Treaty on Plant Genetic Resources for Food and Agriculture (2001) established a multilateral system for open access to plant genetic resources, a principle of Farmers' Rights recognizing farmers as holders of traditional knowledge and stewards of agricultural biodiversity, and conservation obligations covering on-farm conservation and seed banks. TWAIL scholarship examines the 'package deal' and the treaty's future through the lens of negotiated sovereignty, while other work debates what kind of goods plant genetic resources are and whether a new global commons framework is needed.

Mainstream / Institutionalist

The Plant Treaty is a successful example of multilateral cooperation that balances access to genetic resources with benefit-sharing and food security, and its multilateral system provides predictable open access for breeders and researchers. Farmers' Rights and conservation obligations are integrated into the treaty framework, and the priority is to strengthen implementation and adapt the treaty to evolving needs.
