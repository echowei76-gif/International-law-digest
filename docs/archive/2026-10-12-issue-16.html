Genocidal Intent and Military Purpose Justification in South Africa v. Israel and The Gambia v. Myanmar

The ICJ is hearing two genocide cases: South Africa v. Israel concerning Gaza, and The Gambia v. Myanmar concerning the Rohingya. In both, the Court must assess whether military purpose justifications negate genocidal intent, and what remedies are appropriate. The cases have progressed through written pleadings and provisional measures, with the ICJ ordering Israel and Myanmar to take steps to prevent genocidal acts. The central legal question is how to infer intent from patterns of conduct and whether military objectives can excuse otherwise prohibited acts.

Mainstream / Institutionalist

The Genocide Convention requires a specific intent to destroy a protected group, and military purpose alone does not negate such intent if the evidence shows a broader campaign of destruction. The ICJ's provisional measures are binding, and compliance is expected. The Court's proceedings are judicial, not political, and both cases will be decided on the evidence and legal arguments presented. The involvement of states like the US as interveners reflects the importance of the Convention and the need for careful legal analysis of intent.

Critics highlight that ISDS proceedings are difficult to dismiss early, lack statutes of limitation, and pressure lower-resourced states to settle.

Mainstream / Institutionalist

ISDS is a vital mechanism for protecting foreign investment and the rule of law. While reforms are needed to improve efficiency and legitimacy, they should not undermine the system's core function. The UNCITRAL Working Group III process is the appropriate forum for incremental, consensus-based reforms. The recent focus on interest calculation is a technical matter that can be addressed through clearer legal standards, ensuring that awards are fair and predictable. Criticisms of bias are overstated; arbitrators are independent and apply the law objectively.
