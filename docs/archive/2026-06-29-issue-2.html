Climate Justice and Procedural Rights in International Courts

International courts, particularly the European Court of Human Rights and the Inter-American Court of Human Rights, have recently issued landmark rulings linking climate change to human rights obligations, emphasizing procedural rights such as standing, access to justice, and states' positive duties to protect against climate harms. The ECtHR's 2024 KlimaSeniorinnen judgment found Switzerland violated Article 8 (private and family life) and Article 6 (access to court) for inadequate climate mitigation.

Mainstream / Institutionalist

These rulings represent a progressive evolution of human rights law, affirming that states have procedural and substantive duties to address climate change, and that courts can legitimately enforce these obligations within existing human rights frameworks, thereby strengthening the rule of law and accountability.

In February 2026, Greece signed offshore lease agreements with a Chevron-led consortium for oil and gas exploration south of Crete, including a novel clause that compensates the investor if a future maritime delimitation agreement with neighboring states reduces Greece's jurisdiction over the contracted area. This contractual allocation of delimitation risk raises questions about sovereignty and investment protection.

Mainstream / Institutionalist
